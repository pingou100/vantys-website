From legacy to Data-Driven Decisions: Marketing Mix Modelling in Life Sciences

Global BiopharmaceuticalMulti-channel OptimisationAdvanced AnalyticsCapability Building

The Transformation Pathway

The Challenge

When budget decisions are driven by legacy and organisation in place

A global biopharmaceutical company operating across multiple therapeutic areas was investing heavily across sales force, digital and events — but had no reliable way to know which channels were actually driving impact. Budget decisions were made on historical precedent and organisational design rather than evidence. Senior leaders disagreed on the value of the different channels, yet most continued to be funded at similar levels with little data to arbitrate.

As the organisation moved towards omnichannel engagement, attribution became harder. Field teams were overcalling certain HCP segments, underfunding high-potential accounts, and Marketing was deploying digital channels with no plans to measure return. Each annual planning cycle reopened the same unresolved debate: where should we invest to deliver our ambition?

What was missing was not ambition or investment — it was the analytical infrastructure to connect commercial spend to commercial outcomes, and a shared language for leaders to make resource decisions with confidence.

Our Approach

Building the analytical engine behind smarter commercial decisions

A structured approach for sustainable results

  • Commercial Excellence FoundationBefore modelling, we put in place a structured health check approach of commercial excellence fundamentals — segmentation and targeting, Account Planning and execution, call plan coverage, channel mix allocation analysis. Descriptive analytics established a clear baseline of what was actually happening across the promotional mix, surfacing significant gaps between intended strategy and actual execution, and building leadership credibility for the analytical work that followed.
  • Marketing Mix ModellingWe deployed a multi-channel attribution model that decomposed sales into their constituent drivers: field force detailing, meetings and events, digital engagement, seasonality, and competitor activity. The model revealed various patterns depending on the analysed brand, with sometimes a significant mismatch between where budget was concentrated and where sales were actually being generated.
  • Resource OptimisationFor each channel, we built response curves mapping the relationship between investment and incremental sales — distinguishing marginal ROI from average ROI. This identified frequency thresholds beyond which detailing or activity generated diminishing returns, exposed underfunded channels with significant headroom, and shifted leadership thinking from how much did we spend to what did the last dollar of investment actually return.
  • Capability BuildingThe most durable outcome was the change in how decisions were made. We amended the business planning process and developed an interactive scenario planning tool with brand teams, enabling them to simulate channel allocation decisions independently. Structured capability sessions reframed the annual budgeting conversation so senior leaders felt genuinely equipped to make confident investment decisions.
